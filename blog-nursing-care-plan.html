How to Write a Perfect Nursing Care Plan (NANDA Guide)

Breaking down the nursing process: Assessment, Diagnosis, Planning, Implementation, and Evaluation.

Published: October 2026 · Reading time: 6 mins

If you are in nursing school, you already know that writing a Care Plan is one of the most frustrating and time-consuming parts of your clinical coursework. A single mistake in your NANDA diagnosis or your interventions can cost you valuable points.

After helping students with hundreds of health case studies — from Myocardial Infarction to Type 1 Diabetes — we've seen the exact formula professors are looking for. Here is how to write a care plan that guarantees a passing grade.

The 5 Steps of the Nursing Process (ADPIE)

Every care plan follows the ADPIE framework. If you remember this structure, you will never get lost.

1. Assessment (Subjective & Objective Data)

This is where you gather the facts. You need both what the patient tells you (Subjective) and what you observe/measure (Objective).

  • Subjective: "I feel short of breath when I walk to the bathroom."
  • Objective: BP 150/90, HR 102, SpO2 88% on room air.

2. Diagnosis (The NANDA Format)

This is where most students lose points. You cannot just say "Pain." You must use a standard NANDA-I diagnosis format.

The Formula: [Problem] related to [Etiology/Cause] as evidenced by [Signs/Symptoms].

Example: Acute Pain related to surgical incision as evidenced by patient reporting an 8/10 pain scale and guarding of the abdomen.

3. Planning (SMART Goals)

Your goals must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound (SMART). Do not write "Patient will feel better." Write: "Patient will report pain at a level of 3/10 or lower within 30 minutes of receiving pain medication."

4. Implementation (Interventions)

These are the actions you (the nurse) will take. They must be specific and evidence-based.

  • Administer prescribed analgesics.
  • Reposition patient every 2 hours for comfort.
  • Educate the patient on using a pillow to splint the incision when coughing.

5. Evaluation

Did the intervention work? Document whether the goal was met, partially met, or not met. If not met, you must explain why and revise the plan.
