VHDL & FPGA Project Help: How to Build an ALU

Tired of ModelSim errors? Here is a step-by-step breakdown of designing a basic ALU and fixing the most common VHDL mistakes.

Published: October 2026 · Reading time: 7 mins

If you are taking an FPGA or Digital Logic course, you will inevitably be asked to build an ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit). It is the classic rite of passage for electrical engineering students.

In our 1-on-1 tutoring sessions, we've helped students debug everything from 8-bit signed adders to VGA display controllers. Here are the most common reasons your VHDL code isn't compiling or working on the board.

1. Combinational vs. Sequential Logic

The biggest mistake beginners make is mixing combinational logic (like adders and multiplexers) with sequential logic (like flip-flops) in the same process block. Use process(all) for combinational circuits and process(clk) for sequential circuits. Never try to do both in one block.

2. The Sensitivity List

If your simulation runs but the output never changes, your sensitivity list is probably wrong.

-- Wrong (Synthesis might fail or simulate incorrectly)
process(a, b)
begin
  y <= a and b;
end process;

-- Correct (VHDL-2008)
process(all)
begin
  y <= a and b;
end process;

3. Missing Testbenches

Professors love testbenches because they prove you actually simulated your design. A testbench is a separate VHDL file that generates input signals (stimulus) and checks if the output matches what you expect.

If your assignment says "verify in ModelSim," it means you must write a testbench. You cannot just compile the main file.

4. Board Pin Assignments

Your code works perfectly in ModelSim, but when you load it onto the DE1-SoC or Basys 3 board, nothing happens. This is almost always because you forgot to assign the pins in Quartus/Vivado. You have to map your VHDL inputs and outputs to the physical switches and LEDs on the board.
